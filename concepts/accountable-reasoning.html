🟢 Accountable Reasoning

Accountable Reasoning is reasoning structured so that it remains visible, attributable, and correctable — not a separate mechanism, but what several mechanisms working together produce.

Not One Thing — Three Together

Nothing on this site produces accountable reasoning by itself. It is the joint outcome of three conditions, each covered elsewhere:

  • Visible — the reasoning can be inspected, not just its output. See Inspectable Reasoning and Traceable State.
  • Attributable — a specific person is on record as responsible for it. See Responsibility Attribution.
  • Correctable — when it turns out to be wrong, it can be repaired without being erased. See Audit & Repair.

Remove any one of the three, and what remains isn't accountable reasoning — it's a partial version wearing the name.

Why It Needs a Name at All

Each of the three conditions above can exist without the others. A decision can be visible without being attributable to anyone in particular. It can be attributable without being correctable. It can be correctable in principle while nobody can actually see what happened.

Accountable Reasoning names the state where all three hold at once — which is the actual bar most institutional and legal standards for reasoning are already implicitly asking for, whether or not they use this phrase.

Where It Fails

When accountable reasoning is absent, the result is not always dramatic. Often it looks like ordinary, functional output — a recommendation, a decision, a conclusion — with nobody able to say afterward why it was reasonable at the time. This is the everyday texture of Shadow AI.

Accountable Reasoning is what Navigational Coherence looks like, achieved, in one specific instance — not a fourth mechanism alongside the others, but the name for what it looks like when they actually work together.
