🟢 Structural Alignment

Structural alignment is the preservation of accountable reasoning, agency, and traceability across the full lifecycle of distributed AI-mediated systems.

Use this when

Use this concept when alignment can no longer be treated as a property of model outputs alone, but must be ensured across agents, memory systems, and human transitions.

Where you experience this

  • When reasoning remains inspectable across multiple system transitions.
  • When authority remains attributable even after automated delegation.
  • When a decision's originating intention is traceable to its final execution.

System Role

In PKOS, Structural Alignment is the infrastructure that ensures Decision Lineage survives the transitions between systems and time.

Beyond Model Alignment

Traditional alignment asks: "Did the model produce acceptable behavior?" Structural Alignment asks: "Can the system preserve accountable reasoning across the entire execution chain?"

As systems become more distributed, reasoning often fragments. Structural alignment provides the Continuity Layer required to prevent Reconstruction Bankruptcy by making the reasoning chain structurally continuous rather than forensic.

Structural Alignment is Navigational Coherence's answer to the alignment question properly asked: not "does this one output look right," but "does accountable reasoning survive every transition the system goes through" — the same distinction between behavioral alignment and a structural guarantee that matters most exactly when the stakes are highest and no single output can be checked in time.
