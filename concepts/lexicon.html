PKOS Lexicon

The lexicon defines the canonical vocabulary used throughout the PKOS framework. Each concept provides a stable reference point for discussions of reasoning infrastructure, governance architecture, and cumulative knowledge systems.

Definitions here serve as semantic anchors. Narrative pages reference these concepts to maintain continuity of meaning.

The Semantic Spine of Understanding

The concepts in this lexicon are not independent definitions. They form a structured progression — from thought to understanding — and determine whether reasoning accumulates or is lost.

Continuity is the ability to carry this structure forward — from understanding into new thought.

Understanding emerges through a sequence of transformations. Continuity depends on preserving this structure across time.

When this sequence is preserved, understanding feeds new thought. When it breaks, reasoning must be reconstructed.

System Dynamics of Understanding

Understanding evolves under competing forces: those that fragment meaning and those that preserve continuity. The balance between them determines whether systems remain below or cross the continuity threshold.

The Semantic Spine of Understanding

This lexicon is not a glossary. It is a structured map of how understanding either fragments or accumulates.

Understanding is treated as something that can be externalized, structured, and continued. Each concept acts as a semantic anchor within a larger system — the semantic spine — where meaning is stabilized through relationships rather than isolated definitions.

Continuity as a Constraint

The central constraint of this system is Continuity: the ability for reasoning to persist across time without requiring reconstruction.

When continuity holds, understanding accumulates. When it fails, systems drift toward fragmentation, reinterpretation, and reset.

Two System States

Concepts in this lexicon operate across two opposing conditions:

  • 🟢 Continuity & Accumulation — where reasoning is preserved, connected, and extended
  • 🔴 Fragmentation & Loss — where reasoning is lost, disconnected, or must be reconstructed

This distinction is not conceptual — it is structural. Systems move between these states depending on how reasoning is handled.
