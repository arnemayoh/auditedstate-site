🟢 State Anchoring

State Anchoring is the act of recording a stable snapshot of reasoning — its assumptions, context, and justification — at the exact moment a decision crosses into durable, consequential form.

The Moment, Not the Record

It's easy to confuse State Anchoring with the artifact it produces. It isn't the artifact. Traceable State is the noun — the trace that results and travels forward. State Anchoring is the verb — the specific act of fixing that trace in place, at the specific moment it matters most: the crossing itself.

Why the Moment Matters

Reasoning changes shape constantly while it's still exploratory — assumptions get revised, context shifts, alternatives get considered and dropped. None of that needs to be anchored; it's supposed to move.

The moment reasoning crosses a Promotion Gate and becomes something acted on, that movement has to stop being invisible. State Anchoring is what happens at exactly that crossing: a snapshot taken, not of the conclusion alone, but of the reasoning state that made the conclusion reasonable at the time.

What Gets Anchored

  • the assumptions in play at the moment of crossing
  • the justification offered for the decision
  • a reference back to the originating PIFR
  • a timestamp fixing when the crossing occurred

This is the same content Decision Memory preserves — State Anchoring is the mechanism that captures it; Decision Memory is what persists afterward.

Why It Can't Happen Later

An anchor taken after the fact is a reconstruction, not an anchor — it's already subject to hindsight, to the outcome coloring the account of the reasoning that led to it. State Anchoring only does its job if it happens at the crossing itself, before anyone knows how the decision will turn out.

State Anchoring is Traceable State's own verb: not the trace itself, but the specific act of fixing it in place at the moment a decision becomes durable — the Coherence half, caught in the act of happening.
