⚖️ Legibility

Legibility is the condition in which a risk has been disclosed, rendered as a stable, traceable object, and positioned in front of a party with the standing and the means to act on it. Disclosure alone satisfies only the first of these three conditions.

What It Is

Legibility is not a synonym for transparency. It is what transparency has to become before anyone can actually do something with it.

Three conditions are jointly required:

  • Disclosed — documented, logged, and made available in a form a competent reader could in principle understand.
  • Rendered as a stable, traceable object — a specific instance, version, or configuration, bounded and individuated well enough that an intervention can be aimed at it and not at something broader or vaguer.
  • Positioned in front of a party who can act — standing and means, not merely the theoretical authority to intervene.

"Stable" here does not mean stationary. An object that keeps moving — a storm being tracked, a system whose configuration keeps changing — can remain fully legible provided it stays singular and is kept in continuous view. What defeats legibility is not motion. It is losing track of the object as one traceable thing.

Disclosure tells us what is true. Legibility tells us whether anyone is positioned to do something about it.

What It Is Not

Legibility is not achieved by disclosure volume. A risk can be documented exhaustively — logs, reports, published detail — and remain illegible if no stable object has been produced for a party to point an intervention at, or if no party positioned to act has been identified at all.

Legibility is also not:

  • a claim that every disclosed risk must be eliminated — only that it must be actionable by someone;
  • a property that belongs to the discloser alone — it depends equally on the object's traceability and the recipient's standing;
  • a one-time achievement — an object that was legible at disclosure can lose that status later if it is no longer traceable as one thing.

Relation to Transparency

Transparency is an input Legibility requires, not a condition it is identical to. Disclosure produces transparency. It does not by itself produce a stable object or a positioned party. A risk can be maximally transparent and still not legible.

Relation to Orientation

Orientation concerns whether a system can locate itself within its own problem-space. Legibility concerns whether a party outside that system — a regulator, an affected party, a downstream inheritor — can locate and act on a specific risk the system has produced. Orientation is inward-facing; Legibility is what makes a system's own state actionable by someone else.

Why It Matters

Disclosure regimes can expand — more logging, more reporting, more published detail — without producing anything a specific party can actually act on. Where that happens, legibility has not kept pace with transparency, and the gap is easy to miss precisely because disclosure looks like progress. Legibility names the further, distinct achievement disclosure is often mistaken for.

Transparency is what makes a risk visible.
Legibility is what makes it actionable.
