🔴 Super-Optimization

Super-Optimization is the condition in which a goal-directed system becomes increasingly effective at optimizing its operative objectives while the capacity to remain oriented to what the system is actually serving progressively declines.

What It Is

Optimization is necessary. Organizations, institutions, markets, technologies, and intelligent systems all depend on the ability to pursue objectives efficiently.

The problem begins when increasing optimization capability is no longer matched by an equivalent capacity to remain oriented to the larger system.

A system may become progressively better at:

  • meeting targets
  • reducing costs
  • increasing throughput
  • maximizing a measurable outcome
  • automating decisions
  • pursuing an established objective

while becoming progressively less able to answer:

  • What is the system actually serving?
  • Does the objective still represent what matters?
  • What is being displaced by successful optimization?
  • Have conditions changed since the objective was established?
  • Who can still see and judge the behaviour of the whole?
Optimization can improve while orientation deteriorates.

What It Is Not

Super-Optimization does not mean that optimization is inherently harmful. Nor does it mean that a system has become irrational or unsuccessful.

The difficulty is precisely that the system may remain highly successful according to its operative measures.

Super-Optimization is therefore not simply:

  • bad management
  • technical failure
  • inefficiency
  • lack of intelligence
  • failure to reach a target

The system may be intelligent, efficient, internally coherent, and increasingly successful at reaching its target while losing orientation to the wider purpose that originally made the target worth pursuing.

Relation to Suboptimization

Super-Optimization is related to, but broader than, the established systems and management problem of suboptimization.

Suboptimization occurs when a part of a system optimizes its own performance at the expense of the performance of the whole.

Suboptimization: the parts optimize while the whole suffers.

Super-Optimization can remain even after that problem has been solved.

An organization may coordinate its departments extremely well, remove local conflicts, align incentives, and optimize successfully as a whole — while the people inside it become progressively less able to judge what that whole system is ultimately serving.

Super-Optimization: the whole can optimize successfully while our capacity to judge what the whole is optimizing for deteriorates.

Related Failure Modes

Several established concepts describe neighbouring parts of the problem.

  • Suboptimization — local optimization damages whole-system performance.
  • Goal displacement — procedures, metrics, or intermediate goals begin to replace the purpose they were originally intended to serve.
  • Goodhart effects — optimization pressure applied to a measure can cause the measure to lose its relationship to the thing it was intended to represent.

Super-Optimization does not replace these concepts. It names a larger orientation problem that can include them:

optimization capability can increase while system-level orientation declines.

The Orientation Problem

A ship may have an expanding horizon — more information, more capability, better instruments, greater speed — while fog simultaneously makes navigation more difficult.

The problem is not that the horizon expanded. The problem is that greater capability does not automatically preserve the ability to determine:

  • where we are
  • what matters
  • what has changed
  • which signals deserve attention
  • whether the present course still serves the intended destination

Super-Optimization is what happens when the engines become more powerful faster than our capacity to remain oriented to where they are taking us.

Why It Matters

Large human organizations already demonstrate the basic problem.

A corporation does not need a single person to intend every consequence of its behaviour. Thousands of locally reasonable decisions, incentives, processes, targets, and corrections can combine into a system that behaves coherently at a scale no individual participant fully sees.

The organization may therefore become better at optimization while its own participants become less able to maintain a coherent view of:

  • the whole system
  • the consequences of its optimization
  • the relationship between current targets and original purpose
  • the conditions under which those targets should be revised

This makes Super-Optimization older than artificial intelligence.

Relation to AI

Artificial intelligence does not create the underlying problem. It can accelerate it.

Increasingly capable AI systems can expand the speed, scale, and reach of optimization beyond the rate at which human beings and institutions can interpret, evaluate, and recalibrate what the resulting systems are doing.

This creates a useful distinction:

  • Super-intelligence concerns capability outpacing our capacity to remain oriented to an increasingly capable intelligence.
  • Super-Optimization concerns optimization outpacing our capacity to remain oriented to what is being optimized, why, and with what consequences.

The second problem does not require the first.

Relation to Navigational Coherence

Super-Optimization is a failure of Navigational Coherence.

Optimization answers:

How effectively are we moving toward the operative objective?

Navigational Coherence asks the additional questions:

  • Are we still oriented to the larger problem-space?
  • Does the objective still represent what matters?
  • Can we see what our optimization is changing?
  • Can previous reasoning still be recovered?
  • Can we recalibrate when evidence or conditions change?

A system can therefore remain highly optimized while becoming navigationally incoherent.

Failure Condition

Super-Optimization becomes dangerous when success at the operative objective reduces, obscures, or outruns the capacity to examine whether that objective remains appropriate.

At that point:

  • local success can conceal system-level failure
  • measurement can substitute for purpose
  • means can become ends
  • feedback can improve optimization without improving orientation
  • greater intelligence can increase capability without increasing judgment

The central problem is not excessive optimization by itself.

It is optimization without sufficient orientation.

Terminology Note

Super-Optimization is used here as a proposed Audited State working concept for increasing optimization capability accompanied by declining system-level orientation.

It should not be confused with superoptimization in computer science, where the term already refers to techniques for finding an optimal instruction sequence or program.

The concept developed here is instead related to established work on suboptimization, goal displacement, Goodhart effects, systems theory, and organizational behaviour.

Intelligence can enlarge the horizon.
Optimization can make the engines more powerful.
Navigational Coherence determines whether we can still steer.
