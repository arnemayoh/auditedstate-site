🟢 Semantic Anchor

A semantic anchor is a concise statement that stabilizes the interpretation of a concept across contexts.

Stabilizing Meaning

Concepts evolve through use. As they move across contexts, disciplines, and systems, their meaning can shift.

A semantic anchor provides a fixed reference point — a formulation that preserves the intended interpretation.

Forms of Anchors

Semantic anchors can take different forms: canonical definitions, short conceptual statements, memorable formulations, quotes.

They are not exhaustive explanations. They are stabilizing references.

Example

"Shadow AI is not artificial intelligence. It is intelligence without a trace."
— Arne Mayoh

This statement anchors the concept of Shadow AI by fixing its core interpretation.

Role in PKOS

In PKOS, semantic anchors complement persistent semantic scaffolds, inspectable reasoning, and decision lineage.

While scaffolds preserve structure, anchors preserve interpretation.

Relation to Semantic Drift

Semantic drift shifts meaning over time. Semantic anchors do not prevent drift, but make it detectable by providing a stable reference.

Why It Matters

Without anchors: concepts become ambiguous, interpretations diverge, alignment becomes difficult.

With anchors: meaning remains referenceable, drift becomes visible, communication stabilizes.

Semantic Anchor is Coherence's shortest possible unit: not the structure (PSS) or the network (Reasoning Network), just the one sentence that keeps a term from drifting the moment nobody's watching.
