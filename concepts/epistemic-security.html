Epistemic Security

Epistemic Security concerns the conditions under which shared reasoning can be examined, trusted to the appropriate degree, questioned, and corrected without mistaking the survival of information for its authority.

When information acquires unearned authority

Incorrect information is not the only problem. An unsupported interpretation can be repeated until it looks established. A source can be quoted without its limitations. An honest hypothesis, a misleading claim, and a deliberate deception may look similar when their origins and status have disappeared.

Epistemic security is therefore not a promise to determine truth. It is a proposed quality of reasoning infrastructure: keep provenance, evidential status, responsible admission, dissent, and revision sufficiently visible that participants can judge what to rely on.

Freedom to inquire; responsibility to promote

Exploration must remain possible without treating every provisional thought as an institutional commitment. The Promotion Gate marks a deliberate boundary between inquiry and consequential shared use.

A contribution entering a trusted cumulative environment needs an accountable address for its admission. That address need not reveal a contributor's identity publicly. It must provide a reviewable route for responsibility.

Promotion does not mean an opinion becomes fact. Opinions, hypotheses, unresolved objections, and dissent may remain available with their status intact. Nor does a declared intention prove sincerity. These distinctions protect both critical inquiry and collective reliance.

When the record is challenged

Unexpected harm, unreliable provenance, or misleading use can justify investigation without requiring an initial verdict about intent. Proposed responses include provisional parking of consequential reuse, subsequent correction or retirement of former authority, and routes for dissent and appeal. Where others have already relied on a contribution, its dependencies may also need review.

See Audit & Repair, Mutation Blast Radius, and Governance vs Control. An incorrect accusation or hold must also be open to challenge.

What the concept does not claim

This is a working design concept, not a demonstrated ability to detect deceit, a new truth-verification algorithm, or an argument for policing private opinions. Existing practices of provenance, review, recordkeeping, and appeal provide adjacent precedents. The proposed question is how to preserve those conditions across long-lived human–AI reasoning.

Trust should travel with its reasons and limits, not outrun them.
