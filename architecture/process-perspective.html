Process Perspective

Who may move, when, under what conditions, and who remains responsible

The Process Question

Systems Perspective looked at the whole network from above. This page comes down to street level, to one intersection, at one moment — because that's where the actual decision happens. Not "does this network work in general," but: this vehicle, right now, at this line — may it proceed?

Every driver already knows the answer without having to think about it. Approach the junction. Be identifiable — plates, lights, position in the lane. Signal your intention. Wait for the light that grants you the right of way. Only then, proceed. It happens so automatically that it doesn't feel like a process at all. It is one, and it's the same process PKOS asks of a piece of reasoning before it's allowed to become durable.

approach → identify → signal → receive right-of-way → proceed → record → review

Nothing about this is exotic once it's stated this plainly. Everyone already accepts this logic every time they drive through a junction — identify yourself, declare your intention, wait for clearance, proceed, and accept that what you did is now on record. Promotion Gate asks reasoning to clear the same sequence before it's trusted the way a completed crossing is trusted.

The Exception That Proves the Rule

An ambulance under priority doesn't skip the gate. It clears a different one — a context-dependent authority, declared by lights and siren, that other traffic is expected to recognize and yield to. This is the same logic behind PKOS's tiered Promotion strictness across Personal, Professional, and Public contexts: the gate doesn't disappear for higher-stakes or lower-stakes situations, it recalibrates to what the situation actually requires.

Everything on this page happens because something persists underneath it — the road itself, the signal history, the record a technician can actually go and check. Infrastructure is what that persistence is made of.
