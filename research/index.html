Research

PKOS explores infrastructure capable of preserving reasoning continuity in environments where human–AI collaboration accelerates knowledge production.

Rather than presenting a finished theory, this section documents an evolving research program investigating how reasoning systems can remain visible, accountable, and extendable across time.

This dynamic can be visualized as a divergence in understanding over time: systems either continue to accumulate reasoning, or increasingly depend on fragmented reconstruction.

Continuity Under Acceleration

Across domains, systems are experiencing increasing difficulty maintaining continuity of reasoning as knowledge production accelerates.

Historically, systems such as law, education, and science have relied on the accumulation of reasoning across time. Under current conditions, this continuity is increasingly strained.

The Continuity Threshold describes the point at which systems shift from cumulative understanding to increasing dependence on reconstruction. The System Recovery Principle describes how systems respond: either by reconstructing reasoning at rising cost, or by restoring continuity through new structures.

This research program investigates whether reasoning infrastructure can sustain continuity of understanding under accelerating human–AI collaboration.

Essays

These essays explore practical contexts where reasoning traceability becomes relevant as AI systems participate in decision-making and knowledge work.

  • Learning Beyond the Continuity Threshold
    Learning is not guaranteed to accumulate. It accumulates only under conditions where understanding can be carried forward.
  • Why AI Accountability Requires Reconstructable Reasoning
    Accountability in AI systems requires more than explanation. This essay explores why reconstructing reasoning trajectories is necessary for meaningful audit and responsibility.
  • A Cybernetic Ecology for Human–AI Systems
    A systems perspective on human–AI collaboration, describing collective reasoning as an ecology connecting intention, computation, action, and ethical constraint.
  • Education After Generative AI
  • Existential Fatigue and Interpretive Entropy
    Modern discourse generates vast amounts of information but often fails to preserve the reasoning behind it. This essay explores how interpretive entropy may contribute to a subtle sense of exhaustion within contemporary knowledge environments.
  • From Data Provenance to Reasoning Lineage
    Extends the concept of provenance from data lineage to reasoning lineage, arguing that understanding requires preserving how conclusions are formed, not only where data originates.
  • Human Reasoning Expands Meaning; AI Reasoning Narrows Possibilities
    Human reasoning expands interpretive meaning while AI reasoning narrows possibility spaces. This essay explores how these complementary modes of thought shape human–AI collaboration.
  • Hybrid Intelligence Needs Memory
    Human–AI collaboration produces reasoning, but most systems fail to preserve it. This essay explores why continuity is necessary for cumulative understanding.
  • Interpretive Entropy and the Loss of Orientation
    Orientation within a knowledge system requires visible reasoning trajectories. This essay examines how fragmented discourse increases reconstruction cost and why cumulative reasoning infrastructures may become necessary.
  • Reasoning Provenance in Small Enterprise Economics
  • Shadow AI and the Collapse of Institutional Visibility
    When AI participates in reasoning without visible records, institutions lose the ability to reconstruct how decisions were made.
  • Trust in the Age of Collaborative Conjecture
    Examines how human–AI collaboration can strengthen reasoning when the process remains visible and inspectable.
  • Why Courts Reconstruct Thinking
    Courts routinely reconstruct reasoning behind decisions. This essay explores why visible reasoning trajectories are essential for accountability.

Papers & Proposals

Working papers present conceptual proposals within the PKOS research program.

  • Persistent Semantic Scaffolds (PSS): Interpretive Stability and Inspectable Accumulation in Long-Horizon Human-AI Institutional Systems
    A foundational proposal describing the semantic substrate required to preserve reasoning continuity across institutional systems.
  • Traceable State and Reasoning Lineage
    Introduces the principle that no system state should exist without a trace of the reasoning that produced it, enabling reconstruction, accountability, and cumulative understanding.
  • Dual Representation of Reasoning Systems
    Proposes that reasoning systems require both a structural representation (states and transitions) and a dynamic representation (signals across domains) to remain intelligible under acceleration.

Research Context

PKOS draws on ideas from several established research traditions, including cybernetics, information theory, organizational learning, and institutional governance.

These traditions raise a shared question:

How can complex systems accumulate understanding across time without losing the reasoning that produced it?

One area of related work concerns decision provenance, which investigates how the context and processes behind decisions can remain traceable in complex sociotechnical systems.

Another emerging domain involves governance frameworks for artificial intelligence, such as transparency and accountability requirements explored within regulatory initiatives including the EU AI Act.

PKOS investigates whether reasoning infrastructure — including structured reasoning artifacts, semantic scaffolds, and mutation governance — may support institutional learning under conditions of accelerating human–AI collaboration.

A recurring theme across disciplines is that systems can accumulate vast amounts of information while losing the continuity required to understand it.
