Individual Reasoning

Many people are worried about the present and the future. That is not new. The speed is.

If you are here, perhaps you are looking for some direction and clarity, and wondering whether there is any way to deal with this at all.

Imagine someone from the stone age seeing city traffic for the first time. They would turn and run, and they would be right to: traffic is dangerous. We didn't run, and we didn't look away. We built roads, signals, rules, and licences, so that millions of people could keep going where they meant to go. We still work on it every day.

This research suggests a similar view of the issue: the worry is reasonable, and what helps is shared structure that keeps people in charge of where they are going.

Two questions come up most often: where to find information you can trust, and who is deciding what you end up thinking. The rest of this page takes them in turn.

What This Does, and What It Doesn't

It does not tell you what is true. No tool can do that for you, and you should be wary of any that claims to. What it can help with is something nearer to home: keeping track of how you came to think what you think. What you read, what you asked, what a tool suggested, and what you decided. When the ground shifts, as it does, you can go back and see which of your conclusions rested on what.

That is the difference between information and orientation, and it is worked out in full on the Problem page. You cannot verify everything. You can keep your bearings.

Free Will Is Lost to Drift, Not to a Switch

The usual fear is that AI will take over your choices. The quieter risk is that nobody takes them: that your intentions slide, one convenient suggestion at a time, until what you do no longer matches what you meant, and you cannot see when it happened. An informed choice needs some record of what you were informed by.

The Gate Is Yours

A fair worry about this research is the idea of putting your name to a thought. It can sound like being held to something you had not finished thinking, or like someone else deciding when an idea becomes yours. It works the other way round.

Nothing you explore is yours to defend until you choose to carry it forward. Private reasoning stays experimental and unfinished. Making something public, whether an essay, a post, or a note you hand to someone, is a deliberate step, and it is the only step that attaches your name. It does not mean the idea is final or right. It means you are willing to have it examined. The gate exists so that you can think freely on the near side of it.

Not everything we think becomes ours.
Not everything we do defines us.
Agency is the point at which something does —
because we chose it.

— Arne Mayoh

That is all “promotion” means here: the ordinary choice of what stays yours and what you stand behind.

What Stays Private

Because this is where the fear is, this section says only what is true today.

The design intent. Exploration is private by default. It is meant to be yours to delete, and to expire on its own if it is left untouched. Nothing is meant to move from the private side to a public statement unless you promote it.

What no design can promise. Protection from lawful compulsion. Wherever data is stored, in many places a court can order it handed over, in the same way the contents of a phone can be. That is a reason to be deliberate about what you keep, and to be able to delete it.

What is not yet decided. Binding privacy terms. Who holds the data, under what rules, and with what legal obligations will be set by an independent custodian, with proper legal advice, before the system is released to the public. That sequence is deliberate: the custodian comes first. Until then, treat this page as design intent, not as a promise.

From One Person Outward

A single person's reasoning is the smallest system where any of this can be tried. It is easy to change, and the effects are visible. What works for one person can later help a team, and then an institution. A thought promoted once is available for someone else to build on, and each reuse is another round of the same compounding.
