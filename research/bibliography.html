Research Bibliography

The Audited State Research Programme investigates Navigational Coherence — how understanding remains sufficiently coherent to support orientation, judgment, revision, learning, and action as systems, evidence, interpretations, and circumstances change.

This bibliography is organized in three parts: sources genuinely cited in the programme's published research, each tied to the specific paper it supports; sources read and evaluated during the framework's development that never became formal citations; and adjacent contemporary work whose problem-diagnosis strongly overlaps with this programme's own, without functioning as evidentiary support for it.

Part 3 — Intellectual Adjacencies

Daniel Schmachtenberger / The Consilience Project

Schmachtenberger's work on information ecology, collective sensemaking, and civilisational metacrisis independently diagnoses a problem closely adjacent to this programme's own motivating question — how understanding remains coherent as complexity outpaces any single mind's capacity to track it. A detailed provisional comparison finds strong convergence at the level of problem-description (fragmentation, information abundance without orientation, technological acceleration outrunning institutional capacity) and a significant open question at the level of infrastructure: whether collective understanding, once achieved, can be made to persist and remain revisable as participants, evidence, and circumstances change over time.

This relationship is treated deliberately as adjacency, not evidentiary support — Schmachtenberger's corpus is expressed primarily through essays, dialogues, and institutional work rather than peer-reviewed publication, and the comparison should not be read as claiming priority in either direction, nor as positioning one body of work against the other.

Relation to the Research Programme

This bibliography does not attempt to replace these research traditions. Instead, the Audited State programme explores whether ideas drawn from them can be integrated into a framework for reasoning infrastructure that preserves continuity, traceability, and accountability in human–AI reasoning systems.

The framework therefore sits at the intersection of:

  • philosophy of science and measurement
  • cybernetics and systems theory
  • organisational memory and distributed cognition
  • the economics of intangible capital
  • phenomenology and field theory
  • AI governance and decision provenance

Together these fields suggest the possibility of a new interdisciplinary research domain: infrastructure for cumulative reasoning.
