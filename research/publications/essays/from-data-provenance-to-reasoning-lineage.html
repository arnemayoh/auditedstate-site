From Data Provenance to Reasoning Lineage

We know where data comes from. We rarely know how reasoning emerges.

The concept of provenance plays a central role in data systems, knowledge graphs, and scientific workflows. It answers questions like where a piece of data originated, how it was transformed, and what sources contributed to it — a real form of traceability, letting a system track the history of information as it moves.

The Limit of Provenance

Provenance captures the lineage of data. It doesn't capture the lineage of reasoning. In most systems, conclusions get derived through a real sequence of steps — assumptions get introduced, interpretations get made, intermediate conclusions get formed — and almost none of it gets preserved in any structured way. The system retains what was produced, but not how it was actually thought.

From Origin to Understanding

In practice, provenance often gets used as a proxy for credibility: information from a trusted source gets treated as more reliable, not because its reasoning is visible, but because its origin carries weight on its own. That's a limited form of epistemic grounding. A more complete approach wouldn't only track where something came from — it would track how it was actually reasoned into existence. That's the shift from data provenance to reasoning lineage.

Reasoning Lineage

Reasoning lineage extends the idea of provenance from data to thought. Instead of tracking only how information gets transformed, it preserves the actual sequence of reasoning artifacts that led to a conclusion. This research programme achieves that through Traceable State, where each state retains a reference back to the reasoning that produced it — carried forward through PIFRs, which carry intention, justification, and intermediate understanding along with them rather than leaving it behind.

Epistemic Maturity

Once provenance extends to include reasoning, it starts reflecting something different — not just origin, but understanding. A system that can reconstruct how a conclusion actually formed shows a higher degree of epistemic maturity than one that can only report where its data came from. Provenance, in that sense, evolves from a record of origin into a structure for understanding itself.

Why It Matters

Without reasoning lineage, conclusions are hard to evaluate, assumptions stay implicit, and errors are hard to trace back to where they actually entered. With it, decisions can be reconstructed, reasoning can be genuinely challenged, and understanding can accumulate over time instead of resetting. This matters most in exactly the systems where human and AI reasoning interact directly — the same territory explored in Hybrid Intelligence Needs Memory.

Provenance has made data traceable. The next step is to make reasoning traceable.

— Arne Mayoh
