The Road Ahead

The two requirements for a shared infrastructure of understanding

Years have gone into discussing the "threat" of AI, and comparatively little into the infrastructure of our own progress. Traffic is a useful comparison here: roads aren't made safe by teaching every driver a private code of ethics. They're made safe by building lanes and requiring signals.

Moving from an uncoordinated tangle of systems toward something like a Cognitive Super Highway doesn't require more intelligence. It requires more sensible structure.

A Shared Language

Traffic works because everyone agrees on what a red light means. In the space of cognition, there's currently no equivalent agreement — meaning gets reconstructed daily, and intent is lost to ordinary semantic drift.

The first requirement is adopting a Persistent Semantic Scaffold: a shared lexicon and structure where terms like continuity, lineage, and PIFR function as more than ideas — they become the lanes that actually hold reasoning in place.

Signaling Intent

On the road, responsibility is something lived moment to moment: a driver signals, then moves. Making continuity real requires something similar — a real commitment to structural signaling.

That means moving past the black box of Shadow AI, and requiring every agent, human or synthetic, to package its reasoning into a Reasoning Vehicle before it acts. Signaling intent isn't an added burden. It's what actually lets someone else continue from where a person left off.

The Movement Begins

The underlying coordination isn't a new invention. Traffic already proves people can share a path without constant collision. What's needed is applying that same basic logic — signal your intent, and let others act on it accordingly — to reasoning itself, not just to physical movement.

When reasoning stops being hidden and starts being signaled, understanding starts to compound instead of being rebuilt from zero each time.

More on the broader movement is available at CognitiveSuperHighway.org.

Signal your thought, and the movement will follow.

— Arne Mayoh
