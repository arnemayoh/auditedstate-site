Interpretive Entropy: Orientation Lost, Fatigue Felt

Why knowledge systems need reasoning memory

"You can be exhausted not because you lack information, but because you keep having to re-find where you are."

Knowledge work increasingly produces this specific kind of tiredness — not fatigue from effort, but fatigue from having to keep re-establishing orientation that should have stayed intact from the last time.

Losing the Thread, Not the Facts

The information itself rarely disappears. What disappears is the thread connecting it — why a particular fact mattered, what question it was answering, how it related to the decision it was meant to inform. Without that thread, information sits available but disconnected, and every return to it means re-establishing orientation from close to nothing.

The Cost Nobody Names

This reconnection work rarely gets counted as real labor, because it doesn't look like labor — no new output, no visible progress, just a person quietly re-reading, re-remembering, re-orienting before they can even begin. Multiplied across a knowledge system, across many people, across many returns to the same material, this becomes a genuine and largely invisible tax on cumulative reasoning.

Fatigue as a Signal, Not a Weakness

The fatigue itself is worth taking seriously as information, not dismissed as a personal limitation. It's a signal that orientation is being lost faster than it's being preserved — that the system surrounding the work isn't holding onto the reasoning trajectory long enough for a person to simply pick it back up.

What Reasoning Memory Actually Solves

The fix isn't more information, and it isn't more discipline from the people doing the work. It's memory built for reasoning specifically — structure that preserves not just what was concluded, but the path that led there, so that returning to it means continuing rather than re-finding. This is what reasoning traceability is actually for: not compliance, not audit theater, but sparing a person the quiet, uncounted cost of getting lost in their own prior thinking.

Orientation lost is fatigue felt. Reasoning memory is how a system stops losing it in the first place.

— Arne Mayoh
